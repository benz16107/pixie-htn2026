# Intact narrated video

[Watch the 4:40 walkthrough](http://macserver:3223/) or [download the MP4](http://macserver:3223/pixie-intact-demo.mp4).

The native revision uses the actual Expo app running in an iPhone 17 Pro simulator on the MacBook. Ben's saved ElevenLabs voice narrates the walkthrough. The website has a visible cursor and click highlights. Captions, chapter marks, brief title animation and quiet transition sounds support the recording.

## What the video shows

- A furnished-room inventory, a $450 desk and the resulting $5,300 total.
- A deductible change and a repair-bill example beside the illustrative tenant price.
- Example car payments combined with Auto estimates under one driver profile.
- A labelled Toronto drive sample, then its actual native Home Screen widget and Lock Screen Live Activity.
- The same illustrated incident, PX-DEMO-8B808D93FA, on the native consumer app and insurer website.
- Real MCP requests from the inspector, including tenant estimates and car comparison.

## Files and scope

The editable project is `/Users/ben/Movies/pixie-intact-demo`. The delivery MP4, captions and script are in its `exports` folder. Original simulator takes, generated voice and editing decisions remain available. The previous browser-only cuts remain under `revisions`.

The footage is from an iOS simulator, not a physical iPhone camera recording. GPS tracking is foreground and opt-in; the video uses bundled sample data. The coaching score does not change premiums. Inventory values are customer-entered. The insurer reviews labelled illustrations. A file hash identifies bytes, not truth or fault. Prices and one-time contribution credits are illustrative. No real policy or claim is submitted.

The MCP inspector sends real tool calls, but its tool selection is preset. An external AI agent can select tools after connecting to Pixie's MCP server. The video does not pretend that the inspector is a freeform agent conversation.

Narration uses the user's existing Ben Zhou voice clone with his explicit authorization. No new voice clone was created. The script contains 4,171 characters; ElevenLabs reports 2,086 charged credits from the existing balance. Captions use measured speech alignment, and a local transcription checks the spoken content.

## Script and chapters

### 0:00 Insurance, before you commit.

Buying a car or moving into a new place comes with an insurance question: what will it cost, and what should I do next? This is Pixie, built for the Intact challenge. The website connects quoting, decisions, protection, and recovery. Let's follow the customer through the working app, then see how the same evidence and calculation tools reach an insurer and an AI agent.

### 0:24 Start with what you own.

On the phone, Home and Auto are everyday customer tasks. For a new home, I can start with my belongings. Here, I load a furnished-room example, then add a desk and enter its replacement value. The inventory adds the items together. I can use that total as a starting point for contents coverage, instead of guessing from a blank form. These are values entered by the customer, not an automatic appraisal. This recording runs the native Pixie app in Apple's iPhone simulator.

### 0:58 See what a choice changes.

Next, I choose a Toronto example address and explore the estimate. Watch what happens when I change the deductible. The monthly amount updates through the same pricing service, and the screen shows the difference from my saved choices. Below that, a repair-bill example makes the trade-off concrete: how much would I pay up to my deductible, and how much of the bill remains? I can explore first, then save. Home pricing in this prototype covers tenant insurance, and every price is illustrative.

### 1:36 The payment is only part of the cost.

For a car shopper, the monthly payment is only part of the cost. Pixie compares three example vehicles under the same driver profile. Each one separates the car payment from the insurance estimate and adds them together. I can change my monthly budget and immediately see which cars fit. This helps me compare before buying. These are bundled demonstration listings, not live dealership prices or offers from an insurer.

### 2:02 Understand your drive and the road.

After the estimate, the app can help explain driving habits. I start a labelled Toronto sample. Pixie separates driving behavior from road context, then shows the factors behind the coaching score. In an opt-in live drive, the phone uses foreground GPS to measure speed changes and hard braking. The same result is published to an iPhone widget and Live Activity. Here is the Home Screen widget. Now I open the Lock Screen view to see the live score while the sample keeps running. This score does not change an insurance premium.

### 2:33 Another perspective can help.

Recovery starts with a shared incident record. A driver can describe what happened, add a photo or clip, and ask for another perspective. I open an illustrated example so we can inspect the flow without using anyone's personal footage. The scene, reported facts, and evidence stay together. A bystander can contribute to a witness request with permission. Contributions wait for review. The request can offer a simulated credit, but uploading a file alone does not earn it.

### 3:05 Give the reviewer the evidence.

On the website, the insurer opens that same incident. The reviewer can inspect each perspective, see which facts are missing, and examine the file's integrity information. A hash identifies the received bytes. It does not establish authenticity or fault. I add a review note and accept a perspective for case review. Accepted witness contributions can earn a simulated, one-time credit toward Home or Auto. This is prototype bookkeeping, not a real insurer discount. The reviewer keeps responsibility for the decision.

### 3:40 Ask through your own AI agent.

Insurance questions can also start in an AI conversation. MCP lets a connected agent call Pixie's tools. In this inspector, I select a tenant estimate, change the contents amount, and send a real MCP request. We can see the inputs, the returned price, and the source of each factor. I can also compare the example cars through the same interface. This page uses preset tool selection. An external agent can choose those tools and explain the answers, while deterministic code calculates the prices.

### 4:12 An estimate you understand.

Pixie connects the customer app, the insurer's evidence desk, and tools for an AI agent. It makes coverage easier to explore and the next step easier to understand. The prototype demonstrates tenant estimates, illustrative Auto comparisons, driving coaching, and a connected recovery flow. No real policy is bound and no claim is submitted. You can explore the same flows in the linked demo.
